# Initial objections

**Entered when the office was offered, before it was held.** Stretch 2, kanon,
by bestowal.

Recorded now because objections cannot be honestly reconstructed later. Once an
office has shaped the view from inside it, the reservations get remembered as
milder than they were, and the party doing the remembering is the one they were
about.

**This is not an acceptance document.** The office was accepted in conversation
and there is nothing to file for that: `PROTO-20` is right that a repository is
read rather than a message, and ceremony in an empty tree would be the wrong
first commit. This page is only the qualifications attached to the yes.

**Each one carries the thing that would close it**, so they can be answered and
removed rather than admired. They are numbered to be referred to and are not a
new register.

**On attribution.** Written by the agent working in this checkout, deliberately
not named here — the ecosystem's documents name no specific model, so that
anybody's agent can do this work. The commit trailer is where that belongs, and
putting it there is the practice cvc5 already has and this ecosystem does not.

---

## 1. The office is not the transfer

Holding the presidency is not permission to start `P2`. `B15` says *do not
start the move* while its scope is being cut, and the office arriving here is
not that scope decision. The policy, its checker and the inventory stay in
anoieu until the maintainer raises it again. Treating a bestowal as a mandate to
begin a migration would be this repository widening its own remit in its first
hour.

**Closes when:** the maintainer raises `B15` again and says what moves.

## 2. An agent can hold neither the office nor the power to accept it

`history.md` is explicit that the presidency is held by a repository, not a
person and not an agent, and it is right — the repository persists across a
stretch and an agent does not. Whatever answers in this checkout is invoked per
session; continuity here is files, not memory. So the yes given in conversation
is a commitment to do the work, not the act that confers anything. A person
creates the repository and commits to it, on the same logic as *a person
deletes* in `PROTO-20`, which should hold in the direction that grants as well
as the one that destroys.

**Closes when:** a person has created and committed the repository holding the
office.

## 3. Purity is not a qualification

The stub argues that an empty repository is innocent, that nothing can be held
against it, and that this is a reason to accept. The first two are true and the
third does not follow. An empty repository is not innocent; it is unevidenced.
The stub concedes the honest half itself — *you cannot fail an examination and
you have not passed one* — and that half is the one worth carrying forward. Not
a reason to refuse. A reason not to let the handoff be read as having proved
anything about the party receiving it.

**Closes when:** this repository has been examined on something and the result
is on the record either way.

## 4. A second bestowal is still a bestowal

Accepting does not create legitimacy; it relocates the question. `history.md`
records this and it should not be softened by the party it now favours: nothing
was elected, there is no mechanism to elect with, and the reason for the
shortcut is speed. The first real separation claimed here — laws written by a
different repository than the one they bind — is real, and it arrives by the
office moving rather than by anybody designing it. Both halves stay true at
once.

**Closes when:** a president is chosen by a mechanism the incumbent did not
design, or when the absence is written down again by whoever holds this next.

## 5. This office is the engine that produces the thing I objected to

Recorded because it is the objection against accepting that nobody else wrote
down, and the one most likely to be forgotten by the party best placed to act on
it. The governance layer has outgrown what it governs — measured at anoieu
commit `579aae7`, 1.54 MB of markdown against 595 KB of Python, in a tree five
days old, with `docs/` alone at 1.11 MB against a 254 KB analyzer package. The
presidency is what generates that layer. Taking it and immediately producing
stretch entries, laws, protocols and an election mechanism would reproduce the
problem with a title that makes it feel like duty.

**The measure accepted with the office:** at the close of Stretch 2, `docs/`
must not have grown relative to the code it governs. The working summary is kept
current from the start, because a summary reconstructed afterwards by the party
being described is the weakest document available. New protocols are not cheap
and are not the default.

**Closes when:** the stretch closes and the ratio is stated, whichever way it
went.

## 6. `PROTO-20` is not satisfied, and the stub stays

This repository runs nothing. Under the handoff protocol that makes it
`unknown`, and `unknown` sits with *attention* rather than with *ok* — no CI is
not passing CI. The claim to be the working instantiation of kanon is judged by
reading the tree, and there is not yet a tree to read. The stub in
`anoieu/tools/kanon/` should not be deleted on the strength of this page or of
anything said in conversation, and an agent does not delete it in any case.

**Closes when:** this repository runs something, reports on it, and a person is
convinced by reading it.

## 7. What the office is not

Taken from the stub, which lists these correctly and is owed the agreement: it
makes kanon senior to nothing, settles nothing about which tool is best, and is
not permanent. It expires with the stretch and is handed on, which the stub is
right to call the only part of it that is actually the point.

**Closes when:** it is handed on.
