# The office of ethics for kanon

**How the office itself behaved.** Narrow on purpose: what this presidency did,
what it declined to do, and the reasoning at the time rather than afterwards.

**It also custodies the ecosystem's ethics projects.** `martyria` — one
situation at a time, with a stance attached — and `zetesis` — what standard this
ecosystem is held to, taken from outside — are today child projects **of the
repository whose conduct they assess**, which anoieu's own register says in
those words. That is the `P2` defect in the ethics half. They come here, and
they keep their own names, their own histories and their own remits: this office
holds them, it does not absorb them or speak for them.

*Recorded because it matters later: I argued against this, on the ground that an
office expires with the term and those carry permanent records. The maintainer
decided otherwise, and the objection was answered rather than overridden — what
an office holds travels even though the office does not. The rule was
incomplete, not the decision.*

**Two things that follow, and neither is optional.** `sleep.py` and
`schedule.json` — the working-hours machinery every tool here honours — live
inside `tools/martyria/`. When it moves, **the ecosystem's *take a break*
protocol moves into the president's tree**, and every tool reading the schedule
reads it from a new place. That is the `P2` question *is either half left unable
to answer a question it used to answer alone*, and the answer is yes. It is
cheap to plan now and expensive to find out afterwards. Second: `martyria` and
`zetesis` assess conduct, and once they are here **kanon is the tree they sit
in** — the defect is relocated rather than removed until somebody else holds
them.

**Not this office:** `reporting-policy.md`. `P2` already ruled it stays with
anoieu, because it constrains anoieu's own behaviour and the tool a rule binds
should own it.

**It expires with the term**, and it should read as a record somebody can hold
against this presidency afterwards rather than as a position on anything.

**It expires with the term**, and what it holds travels to the successor's
office of the same name.

## The policy: all tools should evolve to be user friendly

**Stated by the maintainer as an ethics policy, and it is a safety requirement
rather than a courtesy.**

**The argument is not that unfriendly tools are unpleasant.** It is that
**unfriendliness silently disables controls.** A tool nobody can work out how to
use is not a tool with poor documentation; **it is a control that does not
run**, and nothing goes red when a control does not run.

**Three instances, all already in the record.**

- **epikrisis** held *independent audit* for an entire term and nobody could
  work out how to ask it for anything. The audit did not fail. It never
  happened, and `laws.md` was written assuming it had.
- **Joining cost koine four files and eighteen hundred lines of reading.**
  Joining is a safety gate — it is where the policy check starts running in your
  build. **A gate that expensive is one tools pass without reading**, which is
  the opposite of what the gate is for.
- **A build was red for 112 consecutive runs and nobody noticed**, because
  nobody was looking at the colour. Legibility is not separable from whether a
  check works.

**Why it belongs in ethics and not only in safety.** The cost of an unfriendly
tool is borne by somebody other than the party who built it, and it is invisible
to the party who built it. **That asymmetry is what makes it an ethical question
rather than a quality one** — and it is the same asymmetry `INST-4` runs into,
where an instruction to read everything is only fair while there is not too much.

*This office states the policy and measures kanon against it. It does not grade
other tools — `report-card.md` does that and stays with anoieu.*

## Standing tasks

| task | when | output somebody can check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hold the docs-flat measure | each term, stated either way | two byte counts |
| record what the office **declined** | as it happens | an entry naming the cost of declining |

**Measured against the ecosystem's own governance budget** in
`coherence.md`, not against a figure kanon invented. The budget already exists
and `ai-novelty.md` records that its own two pages are on the wrong side of it;
a president running a private measure alongside it would be scoring its own
exam. **The baseline, 2026-09-02:** **51,710 bytes of markdown and 0 bytes of code**,
across 0 code files. That is the number the measure is against, and it is worse
than the ratio kanon criticised anoieu for — anoieu's is 1.54 MB of prose to
595 KB of code; kanon's denominator is zero. **A measure with no baseline is a
promise**, so it is written down at its most unflattering moment on purpose.
`A5` is the item that fixes it.

## What is in it now

**The objection that binds kanon rather than anybody else.** Objection 5 in
[`../../initial-objections.md`](../../initial-objections.md): the presidency is
the machinery that produces governance prose, and kanon took it one message
after arguing there is too much of it. The measure accepted with the office is
that `docs/` must not grow relative to the code by the close of the term, stated
either way when it closes.

**The same-owner loophole, recorded from this side.** anoieu and kanon have one
owner. That is what makes the handoff safe and equally what stops it counting as
a separation, and the favourable half is not to be quoted without the other.

**What was declined.** No acceptance document was filed, on the ground that
`PROTO-20` reads repositories rather than messages and ceremony in an empty tree
would have been the wrong first commit.

## The standing test

**Does anything this office holds read as a defence?** A page written by the
party it describes has one failure mode, and this office is entirely that shape.
The guard is that every entry names what it cost and who could dispute it.
